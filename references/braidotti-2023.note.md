# Braidotti et al. 2023 — verification note

**Citation.** M. C. Braidotti, A. Vinante, M. Cromb, A. Sandakumar, D. Faccio and H. Ulbricht, "Amplification of electromagnetic waves by a rotating body", arXiv:2310.12191v1 (18 October 2023), with supplementary material. Read in full from the PDF supplied on 18 October 2026. Journal publication status not checked; confirm before citing.

**Not the paper requested.** It was supplied as "Zel'dovich 1971" but is a 2023 experimental paper that cites Zel'dovich, JETP Lett. 14, 180 (1971), as its reference [1]. The original stays unread; B3's citation of Zel'dovich 1971 keeps its (verify) mark. The title we list for the original ("Generation of waves by a rotating body") is not confirmed by this paper, which gives only the journal reference.

**What it says that bears on our text.**
- Zel'dovich predicted in 1971 that waves scattered by a rotating absorber, such as a metal cylinder, are amplified when ω − ℓΩ < 0, that is, when the body rotates faster than the wave's pattern speed. The rotating body "loses part of its rotational energy to the outgoing waves". He also showed that the effect would amplify vacuum fluctuations, giving spontaneous emission that slows the rotation.
- They report the first electromagnetic observation: a 2 cm aluminium cylinder spun up to 500 Hz in the gap of an LC toroid shows a negative induced resistance once Ω > ω, up to 75 standard deviations from zero at f₀ = 330 Hz. This is amplification of the electromagnetic mode.
- The only previous measurement was an acoustic analogue (Cromb et al., Nat. Phys. 16, 1069 (2020)). They link the effect to induction generators.

**Use for B3.** B3 says rotational energy "can also be drawn off by superradiance, the amplification of waves by a rotating body [Zel'dovich 1971; Torres et al. 2017]". This paper supports that statement as a secondary source for Zel'dovich's prediction and as an electromagnetic confirmation. It can be cited alongside Zel'dovich, not instead of the primary source for the prediction.
