# Ubachs et al. 2016 — verification note

**Citation.** W. Ubachs, J. Bagdonaite, E. J. Salumbides, M. T. Murphy and L. Kaper, "Search for a drifting proton–electron mass ratio from H₂", Rev. Mod. Phys. 88, 021003 (2016). Read from arXiv:1511.04476v1 (13 Nov 2015), full text including reference list. The journal reference comes from the bibliography listing and is not printed in the preprint; confirm at citation time.

**Cited by.** E4 (§7 / §5.1).

**Claim our text makes.** *Particle-mass ratios are constrained to parts per million over billions of years by quasar absorption spectra.*

**What the paper says.**
- Abstract and conclusion: the ten quasar H₂ absorption systems analysed give a weighted average Δμ/μ = (3.1 ± 1.6) × 10⁻⁶ for z = 2.0–4.2, interpreted conservatively as a null result, |Δμ/μ| < 5 × 10⁻⁶ (3σ). "The proton–electron mass ratio has changed by less than 0.0005% for look-back times of 10–12.4 billion years."
- Radio methods at lower redshift are tighter: methanol toward PKS1830−211 at z = 0.89 gives |Δμ/μ| at the level of 10⁻⁷ (1σ); ammonia gives 3σ limits of order 10⁻⁶ at z = 0.69 and 0.89 (§VII.D).
- Laboratory clocks constrain the present-day rate of change separately; the review notes these probe a different cosmological epoch, so the two kinds of limit are complementary, not interchangeable (§II).

**Assessment.** Supported. "Parts per million over billions of years" matches the H₂ result (5 ppm, 3σ, over 10–12.4 Gyr). The tighter methanol limit (about 0.1 ppm over about 7 Gyr) could be added if a stronger statement is wanted.

**Mismatches.** None.

**Status.** Verified 18 October 2026.
