# Arminjon 2004 ("Gravity as Archimedes' thrust") — verification note

**Citation.** M. Arminjon, "Gravity as Archimedes' thrust and a bifurcation in that theory", Found. Phys. 34, 1703 (2004) (confirmed by Arminjon 2006, ref. 22); arXiv:physics/0404103 (v3). Read 21 October 2026 via alphaXiv: pp. 1, 3–10, 12–14, 16, 19–20, 22. Full text not stored.

**Cited in 3.12.** §10.2, What is inherited (Arminjon's scalar ether theory: a fluid ether as the only substance).

**What it gives.**
1. *History:* Euler's interpretation of Newtonian gravity as the pressure gradient of a fluid ether, the pressure falling toward a mass.
2. *The "constitutive ether"* (after Romani): particles are organized flows, vortices, of the one fluid. Close to the Ocean's "no particles, only patterns".
3. *Compressible ether gives gravitational waves* travelling at the ether's sound speed; incompressible gives Newton.
4. *Clocks slow and rods contract* in the ratio of local to asymptotic ether density; the "bifurcation" is between contraction along the gravity direction only and isotropic contraction.
5. *Precedents named:* Romani (gravity as an ether-density gradient with light bending as in a variable-index medium), Podlaha & Sjödin.

**Bearing on 3.9.** In Arminjon the ether density and pressure *decrease* toward a mass and bodies are pushed; in W2 the Ocean is *compressed* near a star and bodies fall by refraction. The sign is opposite and the mechanism different, which L1 must say.
