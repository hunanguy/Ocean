# Deason et al. 2019, local escape speed from Gaia — verification note

**Citation.** A. J. Deason, A. Fattahi, V. Belokurov, N. W. Evans, R. J. J. Grand, F. Marinacci and R. Pakmor, "The local high velocity tail and the Galactic escape speed", Mon. Not. R. Astron. Soc. 485, 3514 (2019); arXiv:1901.02016 (v2, 28 February 2019). Journal reference confirmed 2 November 2026 (ADS 2019MNRAS.485.3514D). Read 25 October 2026 via alphaXiv: abstract and §1 (pp. 1–2), §2.1–2.2, §4.1–4.2.1 and Figs. 8–9 (pp. 8–10), §5 and §6 (pp. 10–12).

**Cited by.** Item 2 of the v3.11 working list (S12). Cited in 3.13: §6 (the eddy's edge).

**What the paper finds.**
1. Sample: about 2,300 counter-rotating halo stars from Gaia DR2 within 3 kpc of the Sun, parallax errors below 10%; about 240 with total speed above 300 km/s. Counter-rotating stars only, to exclude the disk.
2. Local escape speed at r₀ = 8.3 kpc: 528 (+24, −25) km/s, with a prior on the tail shape (1 < k < 2.5) suited to the radially anisotropic halo. With the earlier prior (2.3 < k < 3.7): 580 ± 32 km/s.
3. Total mass within 200 times the critical density: 1.00 (+0.31, −0.24) × 10¹² M☉ combined with v_c(r₀) = 230 km/s.
4. Most Gaia "extreme velocity" candidates are bound halo stars, not escapees.

**Bearing on the Ocean.** Bound counter-rotating stars at 300–500 km/s near the Sun require a potential well far deeper than the visible matter provides (escape speed from the visible matter alone: about 240 km/s in R20's toy model). A force that does no work cannot bind them. See S12. In 3.13 the escape speed places the eddy's edge at about 150–350 kpc in the model Milky Way.
