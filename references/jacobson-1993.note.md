# Jacobson 1993 — Black hole radiation in the presence of a short distance cutoff

**Citation.** T. Jacobson, "Black hole radiation in the presence of a short distance cutoff", Phys. Rev. D 48, 728 (1993); arXiv:hep-th/9303103 (bibcode 1993PhRvD..48..728J, from NASA ADS). Read 11 November 2026 via alphaXiv: abstract, §§1, 3, 4 (4.1–4.5), 5.1, 6.1–6.2, 7, Appendix D.

**What the paper says.**
- Hawking radiation peaks at wavelengths of order M, yet every derivation calls on arbitrarily short distances: late-time outgoing waves trace back to incoming frequencies growing as e^{u/4M}, past the Planck frequency after "only several light crossing times".
- His derivation replaces the initial condition with a boundary condition imposed near the horizon *for all times*: outgoing modes of frequency much above 1/M but below a cutoff ω_c, as seen by **free-fall observers who fell in from rest far away**, are in their ground state. That alone gives the thermal spectrum at κ/2π (his Eq. 33); precision limited to about √(l_c/M).
- The condition cannot be derived from the state before the hole forms within a cutoff theory: "the state of the outgoing field modes at low frequencies descends from presently unknown physics at very high frequency (in the free-fall frame)". If it fails, the spectrum could differ from Hawking's.
- Near the horizon, on a surface of constant advanced time, the horizon coordinate U is linear in r − 2M (Appendix D).
