# Andersson & Comer 2021, relativistic fluid dynamics — verification note

**Citation.** N. Andersson and G. L. Comer, "Relativistic fluid dynamics: physics for many different scales", Living Reviews in Relativity 24, 3 (2021); DOI 10.1007/s41114-021-00031-6 (confirmed from the Southampton eprints record, 27 October 2026); arXiv:2008.12069. Read 27 October 2026 via alphaXiv: pp. 1, 53, 128, 134, 137–140, 142–143, 151–152.

**Cited in 3.12.** §11.8, Frame dragging (quantized lines of the phase, each with circulation hc₀²/μ).

**In 3.12.** The lines are the alternative §11.8 sets aside. They carry the turning only when read over at least one line spacing, a reading scale that is assumed, and are fine enough only if the phase turns at 10 keV or more, which no stiff Ocean allows. Frame dragging is carried by the re-made tilt, with no whirl of the phase.

**What the paper gives.**
1. A relativistic fluid's momentum per particle is μ_a = μu_a (Eq. 5.18, p. 53), conjugate to the particle flux; vorticity is defined from it, ω_ab = 2∇_[a μ_b] (Eq. 5.23).
2. "It is worth emphasizing that it is the momentum that is quantized, not the velocity" (p. 138; also p. 134).
3. For a broken-U(1) (superfluid) description the momentum is the gradient of the phase, μ^n_a = ∇_a φ, and the chemical potential in a frame is μ = −u^a ∇_a φ (Eqs. 13.64, 13.68, p. 139).
4. Onsager–Feynman quantization: the circulation of the canonical momentum, ∮ p_i dl^i = ħ∮∇_i φ dl^i, is quantized; with averaging, vortex areal density N and quantum κ give W = Nκ (Eqs. 13.71–13.72, p. 140; κ = h/2m there for paired neutrons). Relativistic form W^A = Nκ^A (Eq. 13.163, p. 151).
5. Averaging over the vortex array (a smooth rotating fluid) applies "as long as the rotation is sufficiently fast that one can meaningfully average over the vortex array"; "one may also envisage cosmological settings, e.g., involving dark matter condensates (Harko 2011), where averaging is not possible" (p. 140).

**Bearing on the Ocean.** The quantized quantity is the circulation of μu, the phase gradient, not of u. With the paper's u = −∇θ/μ, one turn of the phase gives a circulation of velocity hc²/μ, which is h/m only when μ is the rest energy mc² of the condensing quanta. Item 5 is the granularity question in item 1's "What it requires": a pattern reads a smooth turning only on scales well above the line spacing.
