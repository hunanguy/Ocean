# Barbado, Barceló & Garay 2011 — Hawking radiation as perceived by different observers

**Citation.** L. C. Barbado, C. Barceló and L. J. Garay, "Hawking radiation as perceived by different observers", Class. Quantum Grav. 28, 125021 (2011); arXiv:1101.4382 (v2, 20 July 2011). Volume and article number confirmed 11 November 2026 from the authors' own citation of it in their follow-up paper (Class. Quantum Grav. 29, 075013 (2012), reference [2]: "Class.Quant.Grav., 28:125021, 2011"), read from the Universidad Complutense record. Read 11 November 2026 via alphaXiv: abstract, §§1, 4.1–4.4, 5, 7.

**What the paper says.**
- Method: an effective-temperature function κ(u) = −(d²U/du²)(dU/du)⁻¹ along an observer's world line (their Eq. 28); a true temperature κ/2π where the adiabatic control ε = |dκ/du|/κ² ≪ 1 (Eqs. 30–31); otherwise an estimate of the amount of radiation met.
- Held still at radius r: Hawking's temperature times (1 − 2m/r)^{−1/2} (Eq. 39).
- Falling freely from rest far away, long after the hole formed: a plateau a little above Hawking's temperature (Doppler), then at the horizon crossing κ = 1/m, **four times Hawking's temperature** (Eq. 52, Δt₀ ≫ 4m); adiabatic control there 3/8 (Eq. 53): "not exactly Planckian, it is not hugely different from Planckian either".
- "Generic freely-falling observers do not perceive vacuum when crossing the horizon": only one with zero radial velocity at the horizon would. The cause is a Doppler blue shift that diverges at the horizon against a vanishing Unruh temperature.
- Practically masked: a stellar-mass hole's few nanokelvin are swamped by the ~3 K microwave background.
