# Davis & Lineweaver 2004 — verification note

**Citation.** T. M. Davis and C. H. Lineweaver, "Expanding confusion: common misconceptions of cosmological horizons and the superluminal expansion of the universe", Publ. Astron. Soc. Aust. 21, 97 (2004). Read from arXiv:astro-ph/0310808v2 (13 Nov 2003), full text, all 25 pages including appendices.

**Cited by.** E1 (§5.1).

**Claims our text makes, and what the paper says.**

1. *Regions beyond the Hubble radius recede faster than light.* — Supported. §2: "the Hubble sphere is defined to be the distance beyond which the recession velocity exceeds the speed of light, D_HS = c/H"; "all objects with redshift greater than z ∼ 1.46 are receding faster than the speed of light." §3.1: this "does not contradict SR because the motion is not in any observer's inertial frame."
2. *This is flow, not travel, and no pattern exceeds the local speed limit.* — Supported in the paper's own terms. §3.1: superluminally receding galaxies "are at rest locally (their peculiar velocity, v_pec = 0)"; "the velocity is due to the rate of expansion of space, not movement through space." §6: "All observers measure light locally to be travelling at c and nothing ever overtakes a photon." Our flow/travel wording is the framework's translation of recession versus peculiar velocity.
3. *Some galaxies now receding faster than light remain visible, because the Hubble radius has grown to overtake light they emitted long ago.* — Supported. §2: "since the radius of the Hubble sphere increases with time, some photons that were initially in a superluminally receding region later find themselves in a subluminally receding region. They can therefore approach us and eventually reach us." §3.3: the most distant objects we see "were, are, and always have been, receding from us faster than the speed of light."
4. *Bound systems decouple from the divergence.* — Not in this paper. Our text states it without this citation; keep it uncited or cite separately.

**Useful for later items (not cited yet).** The Hubble sphere is not a horizon (§3.3); particle and event horizons are both surfaces in spacetime (§3.4); in the ΛCDM model, galaxies at z ≈ 1.8 are now crossing our event horizon (§2). Relevant to T4 ("Horizon") and the cosmic-horizon discussion.
