# Donato et al. 2009, a constant dark-matter halo surface density in galaxies — verification note

**Citation.** F. Donato, G. Gentile, P. Salucci, C. Frigerio Martins, M. I. Wilkinson, G. Gilmore, E. K. Grebel, A. Koch and R. Wyse, "A constant dark matter halo surface density in galaxies", Mon. Not. R. Astron. Soc. 397, 1169 (2009); arXiv:0904.4054 (v1, 27 April 2009). Journal reference confirmed 2 November 2026 (journal PDF record MNRAS-2009-Donato-1169-76). Read 1 November 2026 via alphaXiv: pp. 1–7 (whole paper).

**Cited by.** `claude/eddy-strength.md` (where the eddy's limit lives); script `claude/eddy_core.py`. Not cited in the paper.

**What the paper gives.**
1. With Burkert cored halos, ρ(r) = ρ₀r₀³/((r + r₀)(r² + r₀²)), the central surface density μ₀D = ρ₀r₀ is log μ₀D = 2.15 ± 0.2, i.e. 140 (+80/−30) M☉ pc⁻² (abstract; p. 6, Eq. 4).
2. It holds over 14 magnitudes (−8 > M_B > −22) and all Hubble types, from co-added rotation curves of ~1000 spirals, individual dwarfs and spirals (THINGS, Sa), weak lensing of spirals and ellipticals, and Milky Way dwarf spheroidals; ρ₀ spans 10⁻²⁵–6 × 10⁻²³ g cm⁻³ and r₀ 0.3–30 kpc (pp. 1, 3–6).
3. Profile conversions: log μ₀D(Burkert) = log μ₀D(pseudo-isothermal) + 0.24 = log μ₀D(Spano) + 0.1; profiles are matched by enclosed mass in the core (p. 3).
4. Object-to-object variation below ~30% cannot be excluded; μ₀D does not track the stellar central surface density, which varies by orders of magnitude (p. 6).
5. Constant μ₀D implies M_h0 ∝ V_h0⁴, a Tully–Fisher-like relation (p. 6).

**Bearing on the Ocean.** The matching-rule eddy has a core load per unit area set by g† alone, the same for every galaxy mass. Burkert-equivalent: 264 M☉ pc⁻² for the cored smoothing (0.28 dex, 1.4 scatter-widths above 140), 459–526 for the cusped smoothings. So the limit can be read at the eddy's own center, and the cored smoothing is favored.
