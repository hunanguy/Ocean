# Skordis & Złośnik — New relativistic theory for modified Newtonian dynamics
arXiv:2007.00082v3 (14 Oct 2021). Read 22 Nov 2026 (pp. 1–5). Journal reference not confirmed from a read source; cited by arXiv number.

**What it says that we use.** A relativistic MOND theory (scalar plus unit-timelike vector) that fits the Planck CMB temperature and polarization spectra and the SDSS matter power spectrum (Figs. 1–2), with MOND in galaxies. The ingredient that does the work of cold dark matter in cosmology is a shift-symmetric scalar whose Lagrangian has a minimum at a nonzero rate, K(Q) = −2Λ + K₂(Q − Q₀)² + …: its energy density scales as a⁻³ (dust) plus a cosmological constant; "the density ρ̄ is not (classically) predicted" (it depends on an initial condition I₀). Within GR, cold dark matter "sustains the gravitational potentials during the tight coupling period … and affecting the relative peak heights of the CMB" (p. 3). The dust-plus-constant behaviour of such a scalar is credited to Scherrer 2004 (their ref. 87), and the construction is the low-energy limit of ghost condensation (refs. 88–89). Clusters: MOND studies "report that either a₀ is larger in clusters and/or an additional dark component is necessary" (p. 5).

**Use.** §13.3 "The acoustic peaks" (3.16): the Ocean's phase law (Eq. 39) is of the same kind (a rate held at μ₀ by a multiplier), so its departures are a candidate for the work cold dark matter does at recombination; how much there is is not computed. Cited as precedent that a field of that kind can fit the peaks (Fable, 22 Nov).
