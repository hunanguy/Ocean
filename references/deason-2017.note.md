# Deason et al. 2017, the slight spin of the old stellar halo — verification note

**Citation.** A. J. Deason, V. Belokurov, S. E. Koposov, F. A. Gómez, R. J. Grand, F. Marinacci and R. Pakmor, "The slight spin of the old stellar halo", Mon. Not. R. Astron. Soc. 470, 1259 (2017); arXiv:1703.09230. Read 26 October 2026 via alphaXiv: abstract, §1–2 (pp. 1–2), §5.2 with Table 1 and Figs. 6–9 (pp. 8–10), §6.2 (p. 13), §7 (p. 14).

**Cited by.** v3.11 working list, S30. Cited in 3.13: §6 (the eddy's in-plane turning) and Appendix B step 1.

**What the paper gives.**
1. Data: proper motions from SDSS combined with Gaia DR1 for RR Lyrae, blue horizontal branch stars and K giants, with r < 50 kpc and |z| > 4 kpc.
2. Halo rotation is mildly prograde, ⟨V_φ⟩ ≈ 5–25 km/s, averaging 14 ± 2 ± 10 (syst.) km/s.
3. Little variation with radius out to 50 kpc. The 30–40 kpc excess is the Sagittarius stream.
4. The signal is degenerate with the assumed solar motion and vanishes for V_c = 220 km/s, R₀ = 8.5 kpc. Systematics of about 0.1 mas/yr mean signals below 10 km/s are consistent with zero.
5. There is tentative evidence that metal-richer stars rotate slightly faster.

**Bearing on the Ocean.** The inner halo shows no turning that grows outward to 50 kpc. That is consistent with the limit on the eddy's in-plane turning near the disk (at most 15–19 km/s at the Sun in 3.13).

**Status.** Read and verified 26 October 2026. Journal reference as cited (MNRAS 470, 1259); to confirm with the other 3.13 references if a reader asks.
