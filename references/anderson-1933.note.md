# Anderson 1933 — verification note

**Citation.** C. D. Anderson, "The positive electron", Phys. Rev. 43, 491 (1933). Received 28 February 1933; issue of 15 March 1933. Read in full from the PDF supplied on 18 October 2026: pp. 491–494 of text plus the figure plates. Page 492 was image-only and was read from the page image.

**Cited in 3.12.** §10.4 (the positron, the first antimatter partner observed).

**Claim our text makes.** *Every charged localized excitation has an antimatter partner, observed directly: the positron [Anderson 1933] …*

**What the paper says.**
- Abstract: out of 1300 cosmic-ray photographs in a vertical Wilson chamber (15 000 gauss), 15 tracks were of positive particles "which could not have a mass as great as that of the proton". The charge is "less than twice, and is probably exactly equal to, that of the proton", and the mass is "less than twenty times the electron mass". "These particles will be called positrons."
- The first event was photographed on 2 August 1932 (Fig. 1): a 63 MeV positron passes through 6 mm of lead and emerges at 23 MeV. Its path length is "at least ten times greater than the possible length of a proton path of this curvature."
- Anderson interprets positrons as "secondary particles ejected from atomic nuclei". He does not identify them with Dirac's predicted antielectron, and Dirac is not cited. He notes that Blackett and Occhialini had independent confirming evidence.

**Assessment.** Supports D5's use: this is the direct observation of the positron. Two points of framing. First, the paper's limits are wide by modern standards (mass below 20 electron masses; charge equal to the electron's within a factor of two). The exact equality of mass and charge comes from later work, so D5's precision claims must rest on the ALPHA and BASE papers, not on Anderson. Second, the "Dirac predicted, Anderson found" story, which I used conversationally on 15 October, is historically right about the order of events, but Anderson himself did not make that link in this paper. The paper text should not imply he did.
