# MicroBooNE Collaboration 2025, light sterile neutrinos with two beams — verification note

**Citation.** MicroBooNE Collaboration (P. Abratenko et al.), "Search for light sterile neutrinos with two neutrino beams at MicroBooNE", Nature 648, 64–69 (2025); arXiv:2512.07159 (v1, 8 Dec 2025). Journal reference from the NSF public-access record (par.nsf.gov/biblio/10658495), 4 November 2026. Read 4 November 2026 via alphaXiv: pp. 1–3, 5–6, 8–9, 13, 16 (text, Figs. 1–3, Extended Data Fig. 2, references, author list), and checked the same day against the arXiv v1 PDF (17 pages): every number and quotation below found in it. The Nature version itself not seen.

**Cited by.** §12.8 (the sterile-neutrino test) and §14, from 3.14.

**What the paper gives.**
1. A fourth light neutrino would need a mass splitting Δm²₄₁ ≳ 10⁻² eV², far above the two measured ones; the Z width allows only three light neutrinos that take part in the weak force, so any fourth must be "sterile" (p. 1). The study is of light sterile neutrinos (below half the Z mass); heavy right-handed partners above the Z mass are not tested (p. 1).
2. A sterile state both adds electron neutrinos (from muon neutrinos) and removes them (electron neutrinos turning sterile); in one beam the two can cancel. Using the Booster beam (0.57% electron flavor) and the NuMI beam (4.6%) in one detector breaks that cancellation, the first search to do so (pp. 2–3, Fig. 1).
3. Data: 6.369 × 10²⁰ protons on target in the Booster beam, 10.54 × 10²⁰ in NuMI, about half of MicroBooNE's total (p. 3).
4. Result: no sign of extra appearance or extra disappearance. Best 3+1 fit improves χ² by only 0.228 over three neutrinos; p-value 0.96 for no sterile neutrino (Feldman–Cousins) (p. 5).
5. Excluded at 95% CLs: the LSND 99% allowed region and "the vast majority" of the MiniBooNE 95% allowed region; also a significant part of the gallium-anomaly region and part of Neutrino-4's (p. 5, Fig. 3). Summary: "a robust exclusion of a single light sterile neutrino as an explanation for the array of short-baseline neutrino anomalies observed over the past three decades" (p. 6).
6. Left open by the authors: several light sterile neutrinos, neutrino decay, new dark-sector particles; the SBN program (two more liquid-argon detectors at other distances) will test them (p. 6).

**Bearing on the Ocean.** §12.8: of the sixteen settings per family, the one that carries nothing and takes part in nothing is not built ("a build with nothing in it is not a build"); §12.9 test 5: exactly three families. A light sterile neutrino is a setting with no books, so the Ocean says there is none. This result goes the Ocean's way; a sterile signal at SBN would contradict §12.8. Eq. (38)'s sum alone does not exclude a single sterile state (it goes from −½ to −⅓, same sign); it is the "not built" principle that does, and the same principle requires neutrinoless double-beta decay. The MiniBooNE excess itself is not explained by this result.

**Status.** Read at the pages listed and checked against the arXiv v1 PDF. Supportive; cited.
