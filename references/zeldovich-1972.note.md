# Zel'dovich 1972 — verification note

**Citation.** Ya. B. Zel'dovich, "Amplification of cylindrical electromagnetic waves reflected from a rotating body", Zh. Eksp. Teor. Fiz. 62, 2076–2081 (1972) [Sov. Phys. JETP 35, 1085–1087 (1972)]; submitted 10 December 1971. Read in full from the English translation supplied by the user (3 pages).

**Relation to Zel'dovich 1971.** The 1971 note (JETP Lett. 14, 180 [Pis'ma ZhETF 14, 270]) first pointed out the effect. It has not been obtained, and it is the original credit. This 1972 paper is the author's full account: it explains the mechanism, gives an energy-based proof, extends the effect to a collapsing rotating body (the Kerr metric), and estimates its size for electromagnetic waves. B3 cites both. Braidotti et al. 2023, stored separately, reports the first electromagnetic observation.

**Cited by.** B3 (superradiance).

**Claims our text makes, and what the paper says.**

1. *Rotational energy can be drawn off by superradiance, the amplification of waves by a rotating body.* — Supported. "An effect wherein waves are amplified when reflected from a rotating body was pointed out in [1]". The energy proof runs as follows. For a wave of frequency ω and multipolarity n, the body gains angular momentum n/ω per unit energy absorbed. If the body's angular velocity Ω satisfies nΩ > ω, absorbing the wave would require its entropy to decrease. So only reflection with amplification is allowed, and the energy comes from the rotation.
2. *…by a body that turns faster than the wave pattern sweeps round it.* — Supported; this is the paper's stated criterion. The abstract says "the rotational velocity of the body [must] exceed the rotational velocity of the constant-phase surface of the wave", that is, ω < nΩ. B3 now states the condition.
3. *Applies to black holes.* — Supported. For a collapsing rotating body, the effect depends on the change of sign of g₀₀ in the ergosphere. In the limiting Kerr case, at the best wavelength and low n (1 or 2), "the gain does not contain any small parameter" and "may be of the order of unity". For ordinary bodies the effect is possible but small.

**Related points useful elsewhere.**
- Zel'dovich credits Kapitza with noting that the effect is "analogous to amplification of sound by reflection from a resting-medium boundary that moves with supersonic velocity". In every case the moving medium must outrun the phase velocity of the wave. This is the same family as Unruh 1981 (a flow outrunning its sound speed) and fits the Ocean reading of horizons.
- He suggests that a rotating amplifier inside a semi-transparent mirror could generate waves spontaneously, and that the Kerr metric might do the same. That is the idea later called the black-hole bomb, and Press and Teukolsky's instability.
