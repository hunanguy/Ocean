# Famaey & McGaugh 2012, modified Newtonian dynamics — verification note

**Citation.** B. Famaey and S. S. McGaugh, "Modified Newtonian Dynamics (MOND): Observational Phenomenology and Relativistic Extensions", Living Rev. Relativity (2012); doi:10.12942/lrr-2012-10; arXiv:1112.3960 (v2, 20 May 2012). Volume and article number not stated on the pages read; the DOI is the journal's. Read 25 November 2026 via alphaXiv: pp. 1–2, 18, 35–38, 45–46, 51, 56.

**Cited by.** §6, the eddy's strength: the far-out form of Equation (15) is the one modified dynamics uses (Fable 5.1's validation of 25 Nov, finding C1).

**What the paper gives.**
1. Milgrom's law (1983): for g ≫ a₀, g = g_N; for g ≪ a₀, g = √(g_N a₀) (Eq. 4, p. 35).
2. Around a point mass in the weak-acceleration limit, V⁴ = a₀GM: rotation curves flat far out, at a speed set by the mass alone, which is the baryonic Tully–Fisher relation with slope exactly 4 (Eqs. 5–6, p. 36; p. 37, "Kepler-like law" 2).
3. The baryonic Tully–Fisher relation, slope 4, over five decades in baryonic mass with little intrinsic scatter (§4.3.1, p. 18).
4. The interpolating function μ(x) → 1 for x ≫ 1, → x for x ≪ 1; the "simple" μ = x/(1 + x) (Eqs. 7–8, 42); the external field effect (§6.3).

**Bearing on the Ocean.** Far out, where the galaxy's own turning is far below the limit, Equation (15) gives √(g_N g†) and V⁴ = G M_b g†: modified dynamics' form, with g† in the role of a₀. The eddy departs from it through its second relation (the tie-up), which sets how it gives way to the galaxy inside.
