# Blandford & Znajek 1977 — verification note

**Citation.** R. D. Blandford and R. L. Znajek, "Electromagnetic extraction of energy from Kerr black holes", Mon. Not. R. Astron. Soc. 179, 433–456 (1977); received 1976 July 15. DOI 10.1093/mnras/179.3.433. Read in full from the scanned PDF supplied by the user (24 pages, OCR'd); equations are garbled in the OCR text, so check formulae against the PDF.

**Cited by.** B10 (black holes recycle energy).

**Claims our text makes, and what the paper says.**

1. *A magnetic field threading a spinning hole can draw off its rotational energy electromagnetically.* — Supported; this is the paper's central result. A Kerr hole in a force-free magnetosphere, supplied with charges by electron–positron pair cascades near the horizon, loses rotational energy and angular momentum to the field. The hole behaves like a conductor with surface resistance; efficiency is up to 50% for the split-monopole field and about 38% for the paraboloidal field. Luminosity scales as L_H ~ 0.3(a/M)² of a fiducial L_D.
2. *…and beam it out in two opposite directions.* — Supported. For the paraboloidal field, energy is "beamed along antiparallel directions as observations of both compact and extended radio sources seem to require".
3. *The largest jets so powered reach millions of light-years.* — **Not in the paper.** BZ give no jet lengths. Our earlier draft attached this extent to BZ; it is now stated without citation and could take a giant-radio-galaxy reference if one is wanted.

**Related points useful elsewhere.** The paper discusses the Penrose process and superradiant scattering as other ways of spinning a hole down (relevant to B3/B11), mentions the ergosphere and no-hair theorems, and notes that Hawking emission is negligible for holes of about a solar mass or more, whose irreducible mass never decreases.
