# Arminjon 2003 (celestial-mechanics test of v1) — reading note

**Citation.** M. Arminjon, "Testing a theory of gravity in celestial mechanics: a new method and its first results for a scalar theory", in *Recent Research Developments in Astronomy & Astrophysics*, Vol. 1 (Research Signpost, 2003), pp. 859–879; arXiv:gr-qc/0305078. Read 21 October 2026 via alphaXiv: pp. 1–3, 6–12, 19–26. Full text not stored.

**What it gives.** An asymptotic post-Newtonian scheme; v1's planetary equations fitted to the DE403 ephemeris (1956–2000) leave longitude residuals growing for the inner planets, about 3.8″ for Mercury after a century against a current accuracy of about 0.1″; best-fit velocity of the solar system through the preferred frame about 3 km/s. The author attributes much of the residual to the approximation method and to fitting an ephemeris rather than raw observations.

**Use.** Background for L1: the closest precedent has not demonstrated solar-system celestial mechanics at observational accuracy. Not cited.

**Status.** Read 21 October 2026; not cited.
