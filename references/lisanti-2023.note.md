# Lisanti, Moschella, Outmezguine & Slone 2023 — verification note

**Citation.** M. Lisanti, M. Moschella, N. J. Outmezguine and O. Slone, "A preference for cold dark matter over superfluid dark matter in local Milky Way data", arXiv:1911.12365v2 (16 January 2023). Read 22–23 October 2026 via alphaXiv: pp. 1–2, 4–6, 9, 12. Full text not stored. Cited in 3.8 and 3.9 as lisanti2023 (this note is part of the backfill of older citations).

**Cited by.** §6, §10.4(2); T5 and R20 of the v3.10 working list.

**What the paper finds.**
1. *The criterion.* Milky Way data require a substantial enhancement of the radial acceleration but essentially none in the vertical direction locally: "the enhancement in the vertical acceleration must be suppressed compared to that in the radial direction" — stated as a criterion for any dark-matter model (Conclusions).
2. *Why superfluid dark matter fails.* Its phonon-mediated force is approximately parallel to the baryon-only gravitational force, so it enhances vertical acceleration by the same factor as radial; fitting the rotation curve then over-predicts vertical velocity dispersions near the Sun.
3. *Numbers.* Bayes factor ln BF ≈ 32 for cold dark matter over superfluid dark matter; χ² = 77 (CDM) and 144 (SFDM) for 68 degrees of freedom. Robust to a 20–30 per cent disequilibrium reduction of vertical acceleration (ln BF ≈ 13 and 8); restricting to data within 700 (600) pc of the midplane gives ln BF = 9.2 (1.7).
4. *Data used.* Rotation curve 5–18 kpc from Eilers et al. 2019 (Gaia DR2); vertical dispersions and number densities of three mono-abundance populations of about 9000 K dwarfs at the Sun's radius, 300–1200 pc above the midplane (Zhang et al. 2013); local surface densities Σ*(1.1 kpc) = 31.2 ± 1.6 and Σgas = 12.6 ± 1.6 solar masses per pc² (McKee et al. 2015); stellar disk scale length 2.6 ± 0.5 kpc and height 310 ± 50 pc.
5. *In cold dark matter* the vertical acceleration near the Sun is dominated by baryons, with the halo subdominant (Fig. A2).
