# Unruh 1995 — Sonic analogue of black holes and the effects of high frequencies on black hole evaporation

**Citation.** W. G. Unruh, "Sonic analogue of black holes and the effects of high frequencies on black hole evaporation", Phys. Rev. D 51, 2827 (1995); doi:10.1103/PhysRevD.51.2827 (NASA ADS); arXiv:gr-qc/9409008 (titled there "Dumb holes and the effects of high frequencies on black hole evaporation"). Read 11 November 2026 via alphaXiv: §§I–III, the numerical results, Appendix A.

**What the paper says.**
- The naive derivation traces the thermal emission from a solar-mass hole a second after formation to incoming frequencies of order e^{10⁴}: where the theory is certainly wrong.
- In a flowing fluid with a sonic horizon (temperature (1/2π) dv/dx at the horizon), he changes the dispersion relation at short wavelengths (no normal modes above a cutoff) and computes the Bogoliubov coefficients numerically: the emission stays thermal, spectrum and total (0.02302 thermal vs 0.02336 computed; 0.0005364 vs 0.0005545).
- Traced backward, an outgoing packet is blue-shifted near the horizon until its group velocity falls below the flow's, and is carried back out: it comes from an *ingoing* packet, not from exponentially high frequencies at the horizon.
- Discussion: the emission is "primarily a low, not high, energy phenomenon"; high-frequency parts "adjust adiabatically to these slow changes. If the state is the vacuum state at high frequencies, it remains the vacuum". The cause is the exponential parting of outgoing rays on the hole's own time scale. "Dumb holes ... have no quantum hair."
