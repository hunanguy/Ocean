# Schuller 2020 — reading note

**Citation.** F. P. Schuller, "Constructive gravity: foundations and applications", rapporteur article for the Constructive Gravity session, 15th Marcel Grossmann Meeting (Rome, 2018); arXiv:2003.09726. Read in full on 21 October 2026 from the arXiv text.

**Cited by.** Nothing yet. Read as a lead for "Derive gravity from the medium" (§11; R13, R16, R19, S1).

**What it says.**
- Constructive gravity ("gravitational closure") calculates the gravitational action from a given matter action S_matter[A, G) on a geometry G of choice.
- Assumptions: the matter action is local in the matter fields A and ultralocal in the geometry G. The paper restricts to field equations that are linear in their highest derivatives.
- The matter equations, brought to involutive form, give a principal polynomial P(k). P(k) = 0 is the dispersion relation, which encodes the causal structure.
- Three conditions must hold: (a) P is hyperbolic at each point, so initial-data surfaces exist; (b) the dual polynomial P# is hyperbolic, which ties momenta to ray directions; (c) the energy-distinguishing condition, so that observers agree on positive and negative energy for massless momenta.
- When they hold, the matter dynamics induce the whole kinematical interpretation of the geometry: observers, light cones, particle actions. "The kinematics impressed on a geometry follows only from the triple (M, G, S_matter)."
- Requiring the geometry to evolve canonically between the same initial-data surfaces as the matter gives the gravitational closure equations. These are linear homogeneous PDEs, and their solutions are the admissible gravitational Lagrangians.
- **Results.** Standard Model matter on a metric gives the Einstein–Hilbert action. The gravitational and cosmological constants remain undetermined constants of integration, as in GR.
  - Cosmological-symmetry closure gives the Friedmann equations directly.
  - General linear (birefringent) electrodynamics on an area metric gives a weak-field gravity with at least 11 constants.
  - That gravity predicts birefringence in a mass's field, extra massive gravitational-wave modes and a modified Etherington relation.
- **Parametrodynamics.** Constant parameters of a matter theory can be promoted to fields, and closure then gives their dynamics. The text says closure can "predict the values of non-scalar parameters".

**Bearing on the Ocean (our reading, not the paper's claims).**
1. *The direction matches.* "Matter first, geometry second" is the direction of 3.8's refractive account. With the background state of the Ocean as G and its excitations as matter (the split S1 asks for), closure is a known way to show that the background's dynamics must be Einstein's, if excitations see a single metric.
2. *It does not supply G or Λ.* Both remain free constants, so K1 and G2 are not addressed.
3. *A test for the dispersion relation.* The Ocean's k⁴ term, as imported from the non-relativistic Bogoliubov form, has a highest-order part without a time component, so it would fail condition (a). A covariant gradient-stiffness term (§7.7(1)) has to give a principal polynomial that satisfies (a)–(c). This is a concrete criterion for §7.7(1) and (3). See also Raetzel, Rivera & Schuller, Phys. Rev. D 83, 044047 (2011), not yet read.
4. *Different excitations, different cones.* If different excitations saw different cones, closure would give a refined gravity with more constants and extra gravitational-wave modes. That is the technical form of the §2.5 requirement that every excitation respond in the same proportion.
5. *Parametrodynamics and the free functions.* Parametrodynamics bears on κ(φ), G(φ), c(φ) and P(φ), the "flex". But the text speaks of non-scalar parameters, so whether scalar couplings are covered needs checking in Wierzba's work.
6. *The prerequisite.* Closure needs a matter action. The Ocean does not yet have a covariant action for its excitations (§7.7(1)), and that is the step before closure can be used.
