# Weisberg & Huang 2016, relativistic measurements from PSR B1913+16 — verification note

**Citation.** J. M. Weisberg and Y. Huang, "Relativistic measurements from timing the binary pulsar PSR B1913+16", Astrophys. J. 829, 55 (2016); doi:10.3847/0004-637X/829/1/55; arXiv:1606.02744. Journal reference confirmed 2 November 2026 (arXiv DOI field; OSTI record 22667410). Read 28 October 2026 via alphaXiv: pp. 1–2, 4–5, 7–9 (abstract, §1–3, Tables 1–2, §4.1, §4.3, §4.5, §5, §6, Table 3).

**Cited by.** The paper, §12.7 (`weisberg2016`); claude/law-specification.md.

**What the paper gives.**
1. Masses 1.438 ± 0.001 and 1.390 ± 0.001 M☉; P_b = 0.322997448918(3) d; e = 0.6171340(4) (Table 2).
2. Observed orbital decay −2.423(1) × 10⁻¹²; Galactic correction −(0.025 ± 0.004) × 10⁻¹²; intrinsic −(2.398 ± 0.004) × 10⁻¹² (§4.1).
3. General Relativity's prediction from the quadrupole formula: −(2.40263 ± 0.00005) × 10⁻¹²; ratio 0.9983 ± 0.0016 (Eq. 23).
4. The test is "mixed": it uses the periastron advance and the time-dilation term to fix the masses (§5.1).
5. Other binary pulsars agree, e.g. J0737−3039 at 1.003 ± 0.014 (Table 3).

**Bearing on the Ocean.** Radiation by the two stretch-and-squeeze wave patterns alone matches the measured decay to 0.2%: a check on the first-order law and on the record spreading outward.
