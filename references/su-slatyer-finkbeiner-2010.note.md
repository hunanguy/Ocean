# Su, Slatyer & Finkbeiner 2010 — verification note

**Citation.** M. Su, T. R. Slatyer and D. P. Finkbeiner, "Giant gamma-ray bubbles from Fermi-LAT: AGN activity or bipolar Galactic wind?", Astrophys. J. 724, 1044 (2010). Read from arXiv:1005.5480v3 (18 Oct 2010), full text. The stored copy includes figure-axis residue from extraction; all prose was read, including §§1–9 and the reference list.

**Cited in 3.12.** §8.1, Black holes (the Fermi bubbles: a large release of energy at the Galactic centre within about the last ten million years, accretion or starburst, with difficulties for both). The wording follows the assessment below.

**Claim our text made.** *The Fermi bubbles above and below the Milky Way's centre are most likely the remnant of such an outburst [an outburst of the central black hole] a few million years ago.*

**What the paper says.**
- Two gamma-ray bubbles extending about 50° above and below the Galactic centre, about 40° wide; hard spectrum (dN/dE ∼ E⁻²), sharp edges, flat intensity, north–south symmetric, spatially correlated with the WMAP haze and ROSAT 1.5–2 keV X-ray features (abstract, §§3–4).
- Origin (abstract and §9): "most likely created by some large episode of energy injection in the Galactic center, such as past accretion events onto the central massive black hole, or a nuclear starburst in the last ∼10 Myr." The authors "found shortcomings in each scenario" and expect that "significant modifications to one of these ideas, or some combination of different mechanisms" will be needed.
- Against the black-hole jet reading: the north–south symmetry "has no obvious explanation in the context of an AGN jet", and the wide, rounded shape is "not typical of jets" (§9).
- Against the starburst reading: no corresponding Hα signal, and the ²⁶Al gamma-ray line argues against a recent starburst; §6.3.4 says this indicates that "the accretion activity of Sgr A* [is more] plausible as the origin of the mass outflow."
- Energy of order 10⁵⁴–10⁵⁵ erg; at the Eddington luminosity the black hole would need only a short time to supply it (§6.2).

**Assessment.** Our wording overstated the paper. Su et al. leave the origin open between a black-hole accretion episode and a nuclear starburst, within roughly the last ten million years, with one argument (²⁶Al) tilting toward the black hole. Revised B10 wording: "were most likely created by a large release of energy at the Galactic centre within the last ten million years or so, either an accretion episode of the central black hole or a nuclear starburst; the authors find difficulties with both [Su, Slatyer & Finkbeiner 2010]." Later literature may have narrowed this; it is not cited until read.
