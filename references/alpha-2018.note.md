# ALPHA 2018 — verification note

**Citation.** M. Ahmadi et al. (ALPHA Collaboration), "Characterization of the 1S–2S transition in antihydrogen", Nature 557, 71–75 (2018); published online 4 April 2018. DOI 10.1038/s41586-018-0017-2. Open access, CC BY 4.0. Read in full from the PDF supplied by the user (9 pages including Methods and Extended Data).

**Cited by.** D5 (§10.4(5), matter and antimatter).

**Claims our text makes, and what the paper says.**

1. *The antihydrogen 1S–2S transition matches hydrogen to about 2 parts in 10¹².* — Supported. The d–d hyperfine component in antihydrogen is 2,466,061,103,079.4(5.4) kHz against 2,466,061,103,080.3(0.6) kHz calculated for hydrogen in the same trap: agreement "to about 5 kilohertz out of 2.5 × 10¹⁵ hertz", "consistent with charge–parity–time invariance at a relative precision of 2 × 10⁻¹²", an absolute energy sensitivity of 2 × 10⁻²⁰ GeV. The line shape also matches the hydrogen simulation. The authors note the comparison is model-dependent, because the atoms move in a non-uniform trapping field.
2. *Bound antihydrogen, trapped … at CERN.* — Supported: ALPHA-2 at CERN's Antiproton Decelerator, about 20 atoms trapped per trial, storage lifetime at least 60 h; about 15,000 atoms used over ten weeks.
3. *… and laser-cooled.* — **Not in this paper.** The atoms here are magnetically trapped, below 0.5 K, but not laser-cooled. Laser cooling of antihydrogen was reported by ALPHA in 2021 (Baker et al., Nature 592, 35). D5 now cites that, marked to read.

**Related points useful elsewhere.** The paper states that "current standard-model physics requires that hydrogen and antihydrogen have the same energy levels", and that this test already exceeds the neutral-kaon mass difference (4 × 10⁻¹⁹ GeV), long the standard CPT test. It also cites the earlier BASE charge-to-mass agreement of 69 parts per trillion (Ulmer et al. 2015), which BASE 2022 improves to 16.

**Mismatches.** One, handled: "laser-cooled" moved to its own citation.
