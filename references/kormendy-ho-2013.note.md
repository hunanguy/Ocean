# Kormendy & Ho 2013, coevolution (or not) of supermassive black holes and host galaxies — verification note

**Citation.** J. Kormendy and L. C. Ho, "Coevolution (Or Not) of Supermassive Black Holes and Host Galaxies", Annu. Rev. Astron. Astrophys. 51, 511 (2013); arXiv:1304.7762. Journal reference confirmed 2 November 2026 (ADS 2013ARA&A..51..511K). Read 1 November 2026 via alphaXiv: abstract (p. 1), §6 road map (pp. 44–45), §6.6 (pp. 53, 59), §6.8 and Fig. 21 (pp. 63–64), §6.10 and Figs. 23–24 (pp. 66, 69), Fig. 32 (p. 90), §9.1 (p. 113).

**Cited by.** Working note `claude/eddy-strength.md` (Keith, 1 Nov: the speed of the swirl in time sets the center's tightness and whether a black hole forms). Not cited in the paper.

**What the paper gives.**
1. Black-hole mass correlates tightly only with classical bulges and elliptical galaxies, the "hot" components whose stars move with a spread of speeds in all directions (pressure-supported): M_BH ∝ σ_e^(4.38 ± 0.29) (pp. 1, 59, 113).
2. Black holes do not correlate with galaxy disks at all, and only weakly with pseudobulges, which are disk-like and grown out of disks (rotation-dominated) (pp. 45, 63, 113).
3. At a given central velocity dispersion σ, pseudobulges have smaller black holes than classical bulges and ellipticals; searches in pseudobulges fail more often (pp. 63–64, Fig. 21).
4. Black holes do not correlate with a galaxy's outer rotation speed V_circ (the dark-halo proxy) for pseudobulges; for classical bulges the correlation is likely a consequence of the rotation-curve "conspiracy". M101: V_circ = 210 km/s, yet M_BH ≲ 2.6 × 10⁶ M☉, far below what a halo correlation predicts (pp. 66, 69).
5. Bulgeless galaxies host black holes of 10⁵–10⁶ M☉: classical bulges are not necessary for black-hole formation (p. 1).
6. Standard interpretation: tight correlations are built in dissipative major mergers (p. 113).

**Bearing on the Ocean.** Keith's picture: the speed of the swirl in time sets the tightness at the center and whether a black hole forms. Reading motion spread over all directions (pressure-supported) as the swirl in time, and rotation in a plane as the swirl in space, the data match: black holes follow the spread (classical bulges, ellipticals) tightly and steeply, follow rotation (disks, pseudobulges, outer V) little or not at all, and at the same total σ a rotation-dominated pseudobulge, with less of its motion spread, has a smaller black hole.

**The Milky Way (read 2 November 2026, pp. 40, 64, 79):** "The Galaxy has a 'boxy bulge' ... generally understood as an almost-end-on bar. It is therefore a pseudobulge – a component built out of the disk. There is no photometric or kinematic sign of a classical bulge" (p. 40); the Galaxy deviates from the black-hole–globular-cluster correlation "by having very small M•", as pseudobulges do (p. 79); pseudobulge black holes are offset toward smaller masses in all correlations (p. 64, Fig. 21).
