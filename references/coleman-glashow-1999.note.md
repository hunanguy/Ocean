# Coleman & Glashow 1999 — verification note

**Citation.** S. Coleman and S. L. Glashow, "High-energy tests of Lorentz invariance", Phys. Rev. D 59, 116008 (1999) (confirmed by Stecker & Glashow 2001, ref. 3); arXiv:hep-ph/9812418. Read 21 October 2026 via alphaXiv: pp. 1–2, 4, 10, 15–17, 19–20, 22–27. Full text not stored.

**Cited by.** N4 (light and matter must share one speed limit).

**What the paper gives.** Lorentz violation is framed as different maximal attainable speeds for different species in a preferred frame.

1. *If light is slower than the speed limit of matter, fast charged particles outrun light and radiate (vacuum Čerenkov radiation).* Cosmic-ray protons up to about 10²⁰ eV then bound the difference to 1 − c² < 10⁻²³ (§1). This is exactly N4's case (ii): wherever the Ocean is not fully stiff, c_s is below matter's limit.
2. *Electron–photon:* c_γ − c_e < 10⁻¹⁵ (photon stability, 20 TeV cosmic photons); c_e − c_γ < 5 × 10⁻¹³ (electrons to 500 GeV; superseded by Stecker & Glashow 2001).
3. *Summary table:* maximal-speed differences consistent with zero to better than twenty-one decimal places across several species.

**Consequence for N4.** The bound is far stronger than the proposal stated: about one part in 10²³, not 10¹⁵. The present Ocean would have to be stiff to that level, which makes the "gravity disappears" conclusion sharper.
