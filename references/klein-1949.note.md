# Klein 1949 — verification note

**Citation.** O. Klein, "On the thermodynamical equilibrium of fluids in gravitational fields", Rev. Mod. Phys. 21, 531–533 (1949); DOI 10.1103/RevModPhys.21.531. Read in full on 23 October 2026 from the PDF supplied by Keith (3 pages; scanned, OCR read against the page text). Full text not stored.

**Cited in 3.12.** §11.6, The current through time (a fluid at rest in a gravitational field: its chemical potential, including the mass energy, times √|g₀₀| is the same everywhere; the Ocean's chemical potential is its phase rate).

**What the paper shows.**
1. *Two equilibrium conditions for a fluid at rest in a static gravitational field.* Abstract: "apart from the Tolman condition T(g₄₄)^½ = const., [equilibrium is] governed by the condition α(g₄₄)^½ = const., where α is the chemical potential of the fluid (including mass energy)". This is the relativistic generalization of Gibbs's condition μ + Mφ = const.
2. *Derivation.* From the vanishing divergence of the perfect-fluid energy–momentum tensor in a static metric, ∇p + (u + p)∇ν/2 = 0 (his eq. 9, with g₄₄ = c²e^ν), combined with Gibbs–Duhem and Euler relations, giving two independent conditions: T e^(ν/2) = const. (Tolman, eq. 17) and α e^(ν/2) = const. (eq. 18), so α/T = const.
3. *Newtonian limit.* Reduces to μ + Mφ = const. and the barometric formula.
4. *Applications noted.* Cold Fermi gas (Oppenheimer–Volkoff) and radiation; for zero rest mass p = u/3 ∝ e^(−2ν).

**Bearing on the Ocean.** α includes the rest-mass energy, which for the Ocean is the phase rate μ (the chemical potential, §2.2). Klein's α√g₄₄ = const. is exactly "staying in step": μ_local · U = μ∞, so on the spot the Ocean's phase turns faster by the clocks' slowing, while seen from afar it turns at one rate. Klein derives it for a fluid at rest; R14 of the working list shows that close to a point of no return the Ocean keeps step instead by a gentle inflow, where the static configuration fails.
