# Archibald et al. 2018 — the triple system PSR J0337+1715 tests universality of free fall
arXiv:1807.02059 (Nature 559, 73). Read 28 Oct 2026.

**Measurement.** A 1.4359 M☉ millisecond pulsar and a white dwarf in a 1.6-day orbit, both orbiting an
outer white dwarf (327 days). If the neutron star fell toward the outer white dwarf differently from its
inner white-dwarf companion, the inner orbit would be polarised. Result: Δ = m_grav/m_inertial − 1 =
(−1.09 ± 0.74) × 10⁻⁶; |Δ| < 2.6 × 10⁻⁶ at 95%. The neutron star's binding energy is about 10% of its mass;
the white dwarfs' is negligible.

**How we used it.** Any rule that counts less than all of a body's energy toward the draw, depending on
how densely packed the energy is, makes Δ nonzero for a neutron star but not a white dwarf. In 3.12 (§11.9,
§11.10): a density-triggered loosening of the Ocean's grip would act inside neutron stars, so the triple
pulsar places the turnaround density above about 2.4 × 10²³ kg m⁻³, and a fine scale ℓ ≲ 0.035 fm.
