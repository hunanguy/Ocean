# Page 1976b (Paper II) — verification note

**Citation.** D. N. Page, "Particle emission rates from a black hole. II. Massless particles from a rotating hole", Phys. Rev. D 14, 3260–3273 (1976); received 12 May 1976. DOI 10.1103/PhysRevD.14.3260. Read in full from the APS PDF supplied by the user (14 pages; OCR layer, tables and equations partly garbled).

**Cited in 3.12.** §8.1, Black holes (a hole spins down to a nearly non-rotating state before giving up most of its mass).

**Claims our text makes, and what the paper says.**

1. *For a rotating black hole the ripples carry angular momentum preferentially, so it spins down before it gives up most of its mass.* — Supported. The abstract says "Angular momentum is emitted several times faster than energy, so a rapidly rotating black hole spins down to a nearly nonrotating state before most of its mass has been given up." For the canonical set of known light particles, a hole that starts maximally rotating loses half its spin parameter in 21% of its lifetime and half its angular momentum in 6.7%, but half its mass only after 71%. By then a* has fallen to 0.06. More than half the energy is emitted when the hole is so slowly rotating that its power is within 1% of the non-rotating value.
2. *Earlier draft: "before losing much of its mass".* — Tightened to "most", matching the paper.

**Caveats worth knowing.**
- The result is for the known massless or nearly massless fields: neutrinos, photons, gravitons, and electrons at high temperature. Page notes that if hypothetical massless scalar fields dominated the emission, a* might stall at a nonzero value. The spin-down result holds for the known fields.
- Lifetime depends only weakly on the initial spin, varying by a factor of 2.0–2.7. A canonical-species hole lives 2.32 times longer if it starts non-rotating than if it starts maximally rotating.

**Related points useful elsewhere.**
- At high spin, the emission is mostly the spontaneous emission "first discovered by Zel'dovich", the quantum counterpart of superradiant scattering. Pairs are created in the ergosphere, with the negative-energy partner going down the hole. In that regime the area and entropy of the hole first increase, by up to 17.3%, before thermal emission takes over and they fall. This bears on B11 (ergosphere).
- Bosons are amplified in the superradiant regime and fermions are not, which Page ties to the connection between spin and statistics.
- The paper proves the third law for small perturbations: a hole cannot be spun up to the extreme Kerr state.
- The reference list gives Zel'dovich's original as Pis'ma ZhETF 14, 270 (1971) [JETP Lett. 14, 180] and the follow-up as ZhETF 62, 2076 (1972) [Sov. Phys. JETP 35, 1085], confirming both bibliography entries.
