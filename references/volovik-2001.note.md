**What the paper gives.**
1. In superfluid ⁴He the superfluid velocity is the gradient of the phase, v_s = (ħ/m)∇Φ, "and thus the flow of vacuum is curl-free: ∇ × v_s = 0. This is not however a rule: ... the superfluid vacuum flow of ³He-A can have a continuous vorticity" (p. 6).
2. The effective metric for the superfluid's collective modes has the ADM form; "the velocity vector v plays the part of the so-called shift function" (Eqs. 17–20, p. 9).
3. In ³He-A the superfluid velocity "is determined by the twist of the triad ê₁, ê₂, ê₃ = l̂ and corresponds to torsion in the tetrad formalism of gravity": v_s = (ħ/2m) ê₁ⁱ∇ê₂ⁱ (Eq. 95). Its vorticity is nonzero and continuous, the Mermin–Ho relation: ∇ × v_s = (ħ/4m) e_ijk l̂_i ∇l̂_j × ∇l̂_k (Eq. 96; Mermin & Ho, Phys. Rev. Lett. 36, 594 (1976), Volovik's ref. [50], not read directly).
4. The continuous (Anderson–Toulouse–Chechetkin) vortex has a soft core with regular velocity and no singularity on its axis, but the circulation around the soft core is still quantized, κ = 2πħ/m; by Mermin–Ho, l̂ covers the whole 4π sphere as the soft core is swept (Eqs. 133–134, p. 44).
5. Momentum passes between the superfluid vacuum and the normal component through the l̂ texture, which carries continuous vorticity; the resulting force "is usually called the mutual friction, though the term friction is not very good since some or essential part of this force is reversible and thus nondissipative" (p. 43). Measured on rotating ³He-A (Bevan et al., Nature 386, 689 (1997), ref. [38]).
6. Rotation of the vacuum appears to quasiparticles as an effective gravimagnetic field: g_0i = v_si/c⊥², B_g = ∇ × g = 2Ω/c⊥² (Eq. 171, p. 55).
7. Each atom of the ³He-A vacuum carries orbital angular momentum ħ/2 along l̂ (Eq. 167, p. 55).
8. A quantized vortex acts on phonons as a spinning cosmic string: the interval contains dt + κdφ/(2πc²), a time shift on circling the line (Eqs. 304–305, p. 82).
9. In the limit with superfluidity removed, the remaining effective gravity comes from the inhomogeneity of l̂, and its twist term (l̂·(∇ × l̂))² has the structure of the Einstein action (Eqs. 374–378, p. 105).

**Bearing on the Ocean.** A superfluid whose order parameter carries a direction can turn smoothly, with no singular lines, because its flow is the twist of that direction (items 3–4), and that smooth turning acts on its excitations as a gravimagnetic field (item 6). This is the kind of directional part item 1 requires. Cautions: circulation is still quantized per soft core, so turning is smooth only where soft cores are as large as their spacing; ³He-A is fermionic at bottom (the cost 3.10 §7.7(7) already records); the directional part carries intrinsic angular momentum (item 7).
