# BASE 2022 — verification note (abstract only)

**Citation.** M. J. Borchert et al. (BASE Collaboration), "A 16-parts-per-trillion measurement of the antiproton-to-proton charge–mass ratio", Nature 601, 53–57 (2022). DOI 10.1038/s41586-021-04203-w. The full text is behind a subscription and has been requested from the authors. This note is based on the published abstract only (nature.com, read 18 October 2026). Volume and pages are confirmed by ALPHA-g 2023's reference list.

**Cited in 3.12.** §10.4 (the antiproton charge-to-mass ratio matches the proton's to about 16 parts in 10¹²).

**Claim our text makes, and what the abstract says.**

1. *The antiproton charge-to-mass ratio matches the proton's to about 16 parts in 10¹².* — Supported by the abstract. It reports "a new comparison of the proton/antiproton charge-to-mass ratios with a fractional uncertainty of 16 parts per trillion". The result, −(q/m)ₚ/(q/m)ₚ̄ = 1.000000000003(16), "is consistent with the fundamental charge–parity–time reversal invariance". It improves on the previous best measurement by a factor of 4.3.

**Second source (19 October 2026).** The BASE collaboration's own summary page, https://base.web.cern.ch/a-16-parts-per-trillion-comparison-of-the-protonantiproton-charge-to-mass-ratios/, gives the same result: R = 1.000 000 000 003(16), agreement to 16 parts per trillion, from about 24,000 cyclotron-frequency comparisons over about a year and a half, with no sign of new physics at an energy resolution of 2 × 10⁻²⁷ GeV. It also describes the first differential test of the weak equivalence principle for baryonic matter/antimatter clocks, finding no oscillating signal as the Earth orbits the Sun at a level of three parts in 100. The GSI repository record (246018) carries no full-text file.

**Related points.** The abstract also reports a clock test of the weak equivalence principle for antimatter: hypothetical violations are constrained to less than 1.8 × 10⁻⁷, and a first differential test with antiprotons gives less than 0.030. This complements ALPHA-g 2023's direct free-fall result.
