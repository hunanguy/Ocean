# Hu & Dodelson 2002, cosmic microwave background anisotropies — verification note

**Citation.** W. Hu and S. Dodelson, "Cosmic Microwave Background Anisotropies", Annu. Rev. Astron. Astrophys. 40 (2002); doi:10.1146/annurev.astro.40.060401.093926; arXiv:astro-ph/0110414. Page numbers not stated on the pages read. Read 25 November 2026 via alphaXiv: pp. 1, 5–6, 9–10.

**Cited by.** §5.1, the microwave background: the damping of the smallest spots is what other work calls Silk damping (Fable 5.1's validation of 25 Nov, finding C4).

**What the paper gives.**
1. Before recombination the plasma is a tightly coupled photon–baryon fluid; the photon pressure p = ρ/3 gives sound at c_s = 1/√3, lowered by the baryons to 1/√(3(1 + R)) (§3.1, Eq. 9; §3.4).
2. Damping (§3.6): the coupling of photons and electrons is not perfect; photon diffusion gives a viscosity and heat conduction that damp the oscillations by a factor of order exp(−k²η/τ̇); the damping scale is the geometric mean of the horizon and the photon's mean free path; "Damping can be thought of as the result of the random walk in the baryons that takes photons from hot regions into cold and vice-versa (Silk 1968)"; it suppresses the oscillations beyond the third peak.

**Bearing on the Ocean.** §5.1's account of the background's smallest spots, light traveling far between meetings with matter and carrying the sound off the shortest rings, is this damping, now named and cited.
