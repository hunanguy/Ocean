# Baker et al. 2021 — verification note

**Citation.** C. J. Baker et al. (ALPHA Collaboration), "Laser cooling of antihydrogen atoms", Nature 592, 35–42 (2021); published online 31 March 2021. DOI 10.1038/s41586-021-03289-6. Open access, CC BY 4.0. Read in full from the PDF supplied by the user (18 pages including Methods and Extended Data).

**Cited by.** D5 (§10.4(5)), for "laser-cooled".

**Claims our text makes, and what the paper says.**

1. *Bound antihydrogen, trapped and laser-cooled at CERN.* — Supported. "Here we demonstrate laser cooling of antihydrogen." The work was done at ALPHA-2 at CERN: magnetically trapped antihydrogen was Doppler-cooled on the 1S–2P Lyman-α transition with pulsed 121.6 nm light.
   - Cooling was applied along one axis only, but the trap couples the motions, so the atoms were cooled in three dimensions.
   - The median transverse energy fell by more than an order of magnitude, from 15.1 to 1.3 μeV. The fraction of atoms below 1 μeV rose from 3% to 43%.
   - The 1S–2S line became about four times narrower: 14.4 ± 4.0 kHz against 57.6 ± 12 kHz.
   - The authors check the alternative explanation, selective loss of hot atoms, and rule it out.

**Related points useful elsewhere.**
- The paper calls this the first application of laser cooling to antimatter.
- It expects colder antihydrogen to sharpen both the CPT spectroscopy (ALPHA 2018) and the gravity measurement (ALPHA-g 2023).
- It notes that the same technique could cool hydrogen and antihydrogen side by side, for direct comparison.
