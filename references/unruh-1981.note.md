# Unruh 1981 — verification note

**Citation.** W. G. Unruh, "Experimental Black-Hole Evaporation?", Phys. Rev. Lett. 46, 1351–1353 (1981); received 8 December 1980. DOI 10.1103/PhysRevLett.46.1351. Read in full from the APS PDF supplied by the user (3 pages).

**Cited in 3.12.** §8.1, Black holes (a fluid flowing faster than its sound speed has a horizon of the same form and emits thermal sound).

**Claims our text makes, and what the paper says.**

1. *A fluid flowing faster than its own speed of sound has a horizon of the same form.* — Supported. Sound in an irrotational fluid obeys the equation of a massless scalar field in an effective metric; for a convergent flow that smoothly passes the speed of sound at r = R, the metric near R takes the form of the Schwarzschild metric near its horizon. Footnotes note that neither spherical symmetry nor the specific flow is essential: "the conclusions … depend only on the existence of a sonic horizon", and a nozzle gives the same result.
2. *…and is predicted to emit thermal sound by the same argument.* — Supported. Assuming the fluid-comoving observer sees the vacuum at the horizon, the sonic hole emits sound waves "with a thermal spectrum (multiplied by a albedo function … just as for the black-hole case)", with temperature set by the gradient of the flow speed at the horizon, compared with ħc³/8πkGM for a black hole.
3. *Earlier draft: "Laboratory horizons produce the same effect".* — **Overstated for this paper.** Unruh's is a theoretical prediction; he calls experimental testing "an extremely slim possibility", estimating about 3×10⁻⁷ K for sound at 300 m/s through a 1 mm horizon, "probably undetectable" against turbulence. The observation belongs to Steinhauer 2016. B3 now separates the two.

**Related point useful elsewhere.** Unruh presents the model as a laboratory for quantum-gravity effects on evaporation: the smooth-fluid equations break down at atomic scales, as smooth spacetime is expected to at the Planck scale, and the emitted phonons back-react on the flow as gravitons would on spacetime. This is the setting for the dispersion-relation item (Unruh 1995; Corley & Jacobson 1996) and fits the Ocean reading directly.

**Mismatches.** One, fixed: prediction no longer described as a laboratory result.
