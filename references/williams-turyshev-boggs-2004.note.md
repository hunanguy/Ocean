# Williams, Turyshev & Boggs 2004, lunar laser ranging tests — verification note

**Citation.** J. G. Williams, S. G. Turyshev and D. H. Boggs, "Progress in lunar laser ranging tests of relativistic gravity", arXiv:gr-qc/0411113 (v2, 29 December 2004); published in Physical Review Letters 93, 261101 (2004); DOI 10.1103/PhysRevLett.93.261101 (confirmed from the arXiv record, 26 October 2026). Read in full 26 October 2026 via alphaXiv (4 pages).

**Cited in 3.12.** §11.8, Frame dragging (the lunar orbit's turning by its travel through the Sun's slowed Ocean, measured as General Relativity gives it, to 0.6 per cent).

**What the paper gives.**
1. Data: 15,553 lunar laser ranging normal points, March 1970 to April 2004; post-fit scatter about 2 cm over the last ten years.
2. Lunar laser ranging "can also precisely measure the de Sitter precession — effectively a spin-orbit coupling affecting the lunar orbit in the frame co-moving with the Earth-Moon system's motion around the Sun" (§II).
3. Geodetic precession, as a relative deviation from General Relativity: K_gp = −0.0019 ± 0.0064 (Eq. 10). Correlated (0.88) with the lunar Love number and a lunar core oblateness parameter.
4. Also: equivalence principle Δ(M_G/M_I)_EP = (−1.0 ± 1.4) × 10⁻¹³; strong equivalence principle η = (4.4 ± 4.5) × 10⁻⁴; β − 1 = (1.2 ± 1.1) × 10⁻⁴ with Cassini's γ; Ġ/G = (4 ± 9) × 10⁻¹³ per year. "Current LLR results are consistent with the GR gravitomagnetic effect on the lunar orbit within 0.1% of the predicted level" (citing Nordtvedt).
5. The size of the de Sitter precession itself (about 19 mas/yr) is not stated in this paper; from recollection, to confirm elsewhere before citing a number.

**Bearing on the Ocean.** The Earth–Moon system's travel through the Sun's slowed region turns the lunar orbit plane as General Relativity gives, to 0.64% (one standard deviation). In the Ocean's terms: travelling with the Earth around the Sun leaves the expected turning and nothing more.
