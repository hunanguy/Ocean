# Borexino Collaboration (Agostini et al.) — Comprehensive geoneutrino analysis with Borexino
arXiv:1909.02257v2 (Phys. Rev. D 101, 012009, 2020). Read: abstract, introduction, §5, §11, conclusions (29 Oct 2026).
- 3262.74 days (2007–2019) at Gran Sasso, Italy; 52.6 (+9.4 −8.6 stat) geoneutrinos from 238U and 232Th; signal 47.0 (+8.4 −7.7) TNU; mantle signal seen at 99.0% CL.
- What decays lay down (their Eqs. 1–5): 238U → 206Pb + 8α + 8e− + 6 ν̄e + 51.7 MeV; 232Th → 208Pb + 6α + 4e− + 4 ν̄e; 40K → 40Ca + e− + ν̄e (89.3%), or captures an electron and lays down a ν_e (10.7%).
- Detected particle, in the authors' terms: electron antineutrinos, by inverse beta decay on free protons (threshold 1.8 MeV). KamLAND (Japan) also detects them (cited there; not read).
- U + Th geoneutrino flux at the surface is of order 10^6 per cm² per second.
- Earth's radiogenic heat 38.2 (+13.6 −12.7) TW (with lithosphere 8.1 TW and K assumed).
- Use in 3.12 (§12.3, step 9): the visible antimatter of the Earth's rebuild. Each decay lays down one electron and one antineutrino; electron capture picks up an electron and lays down a neutrino.
